DANIEL'S TAKE • NSW PROPERTY REFORMS • 13 NOVEMBER 2026

Price transparency. The days of “Contact Agent” are numbered.

From Tuesday 17 November, new NSW residential property advertisements must display a selling price or price range. I welcome the change, but transparency should be about more than displaying a number.

“Offers Invited”, “Contact Agent”, “New to Market” and “Expressions of Interest” may still describe a campaign, but for new residential property advertising they cannot take the place of the required selling price or price range. The new requirement also applies to auction campaigns.

Just two days ago, I noticed a property newly advertised as “New to Market” without a displayed price. That does not necessarily breach the rules in force at the time. But with the changes so close, it made me wonder: why wait for legislation before giving buyers clearer information?

What does this mean for homeowners?

If you're preparing to sell, there is even more reason to have a clear, evidence-based conversation about pricing before your home goes to market. Your agent's estimated selling price, relevant comparable sales and your expectations all matter.

It does not mean you must accept an offer at the advertised price, or that the final sale price is guaranteed. However, an owner's stated minimum acceptable price and certain rejected offers can affect the minimum price at which a property may legally be advertised.

The rules apply to new residential advertisements from 17 November. This is not a blanket requirement for every advertisement published before that date, and commercial property is outside the residential advertising rule.

My perspective

Real estate isn't an exact science. Buyer competition, presentation, market conditions and individual circumstances still influence the outcome.

It's not just about displaying a price. It's about being able to explain how that price was determined.

Comparable sales, market evidence and professional judgement should be part of that conversation. These changes won't solve every challenge in real estate, but I believe they are a positive step towards greater confidence in our industry.
