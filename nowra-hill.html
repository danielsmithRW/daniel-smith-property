NOWRA HILL • 4 NOVEMBER 2026

Room to move.
And a story beyond the acreage.

Rural space, naval aviation history and a property market where a handful of sales never tells the whole story.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

Nowra Hill offers a different pace from the traditional residential streets closer to Nowra. Acreage homes, open paddocks, established trees and bushland are all part of its character. For people who value space and privacy but still want access to Nowra, that combination is a big part of the appeal.

More than an acreage address

Nowra Hill's housing is varied. Land size, house size, improvements, outbuildings and position can differ substantially from one property to the next. That variety matters to buyers and makes it difficult to sum up the local market with a single number.

HMAS Albatross and naval aviation

Nowra Hill is home to HMAS Albatross, the Royal Australian Navy's only naval air station. Commissioned in 1948, it is an important part of the Shoalhaven's Defence history and its present-day identity. The Fleet Air Arm Museum is also located at the base.

It is an active air station, so aircraft movements and noise are practical considerations for anyone investigating a property in the surrounding area.

What about the Nowra Bypass?

The proposed Nowra Bypass is a long-term transport planning project intended to improve movement around Nowra. It is worth watching, but a route through Nowra Hill should not be treated as confirmed. Prospective buyers and owners should consult the latest Transport for NSW project information rather than assume any particular property will be affected.

NOWRA HILL HOUSE MARKET • 4 NOVEMBER 2026$1.14m

Rolling 12-month median house sale price. Nine recorded house sales, with five properties currently advertised for sale in the local market check.

What the sales actually show

There were nine recorded house sales in the 12 months from October 2025 to September 2026. Six were on holdings smaller than 10,000 square metres, or one hectare, while three were on larger blocks.

The highest sale in that period was 19 Greendale Close at $2 million in August 2026. Other reported transactions include 148 Albatross Road at $900,000, 48 Calymea Street at $960,000 and 26 Calymea Street at $1.04 million.

The rolling median was $1.14 million, down 17.1% against the preceding 12-month median of $1.375 million. But with so few transactions, the mix of properties sold can shift that median considerably. The change does not, by itself, establish that comparable homes have lost 17.1% of their value.

Daniel's take

What interests me about Nowra Hill is how few properties change hands and how different those properties can be. Six of the nine recorded sales were on smaller acreage holdings, which helps explain why I would be cautious about reading too much into the suburb-wide median.

My impression is that this is a relatively tightly held area. People choose the space, privacy and lifestyle for a reason, and I suspect many are content to stay unless their circumstances change. That is an observation, not something the sales figures alone can prove.

For an individual Nowra Hill property, I would put more weight on genuinely comparable sales, land size, improvements and buyer appeal than on a single suburb percentage.
