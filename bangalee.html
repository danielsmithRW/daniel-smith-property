BONUS SUBURB BY REQUEST • BANGALEE • 14 OCTOBER 2026

Small suburb. Big blocks.
Very few sales.

Bangalee is a tightly held pocket close to Nowra where low sales volume makes the individual property — not just the suburb median — especially important.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

This edition came about a little differently. After the Around the Suburbs series started gaining traction, a Bangalee homeowner reached out and asked if I could take a closer look at her suburb. Absolutely. That is exactly what I want this series to become — useful local information shaped by the questions people who actually live here are asking.

Bangalee is one of those parts of the Shoalhaven that can easily fly under the radar. It is a small, leafy residential pocket north-west of Nowra where larger blocks, established homes, bushland surroundings and a little more breathing room form a big part of the appeal.

You can be surprisingly close to Nowra while feeling considerably further away from it.

12 MONTHS TO SEPTEMBER 2026 $1,290,000

Median house sale price, with just 7 house sales recorded across the period.

The number that matters most: seven

Over the 12 months to September 2026, Bangalee recorded a median house price of $1,290,000, median price growth of 2.4% and a median 152 days on market.

But for me, the most important number is not $1.29 million. It is seven.

When only seven houses change hands in an entire year, I would be very careful telling a homeowner that the suburb median automatically represents the value of their property.

In a larger suburb with 100 or 200 sales, an individual transaction has less ability to move the overall number. With seven sales, one large renovated home, an exceptional block or a property requiring significant work can represent a meaningful percentage of the entire year's market.

A very property-specific market

Bangalee is the kind of suburb where land, outlook, privacy, improvements, usable yard, garaging, condition and exact position can substantially change who competes for a property and what they are prepared to pay.

I would also be cautious about treating the 152-day median as evidence that Bangalee homes simply take five months to sell. With such a small sample, I would want to know which properties sold, how they were priced, their condition and what happened during each campaign before drawing that conclusion.

The fewer comparable sales we have, the more important the actual property becomes.

What is happening locally?

Bangalee is not defined by major commercial development — and for many residents, that is part of the attraction. Local infrastructure and access tend to matter more than flashy announcements.

Shoalhaven City Council's 2026/27 road resealing program includes works on Bimbimbie Avenue, while Illaroo Road has also featured in Council road rehabilitation works.

They may not be glamorous projects, but in an established residential area the basics matter: roads, access, drainage, maintenance and keeping existing infrastructure in good condition.

Daniel's take

Bangalee is exactly the type of suburb where automated estimates and headline medians need to be treated cautiously. Not because the data is wrong, but because there simply is not much of it.

If I were appraising a Bangalee property, I would absolutely look at recent Bangalee sales, but I would also look closely at the individual attributes of those homes, older relevant sales, land size and improvements, and what comparable buyers are paying for similar properties in surrounding areas.

The fewer comparable sales you have, the more important the individual property becomes.

And perhaps seven sales tells us something else about Bangalee: not many people seem particularly keen to leave.
