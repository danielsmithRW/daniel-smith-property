TERARA • 11 NOVEMBER 2026

The village
that came before Nowra.

River trade, historic floods, a Melbourne Cup connection and a property market that needs more than a median.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

Just east of Nowra, beside the Shoalhaven River, Terara has a story that stretches well beyond its peaceful rural setting. Today it is known for historic homes, established gardens and farmland. But in the nineteenth century, this was one of the district's main centres of trade.

Before Nowra, there was Terara

Terara developed around land associated with merchant Prosper de Mestre in the 1830s. Its position on the Shoalhaven River made it a natural place for river transport and commerce. Wharves, stores, hotels and other businesses served a growing district at a time when moving goods by water was vital.

It is easy to drive through the village today without realising how central it once was to the Shoalhaven's early commercial life.

The floods that changed the district

Terara's riverfront position helped the village prosper, but it also left the settlement exposed to flooding. Major floods in 1860 and 1870 damaged the town and helped shift commercial activity towards higher ground in Nowra. The development of today's regional centre is closely connected to that change.

Flood history remains relevant to property decisions in the wider floodplain. Buyers and owners should check current flood-planning information for the individual property rather than rely on suburb-wide assumptions.

From Terara to the Melbourne Cup

Terara also has an unexpected connection to Australian racing history. Etienne de Mestre trained Archer, winner of the first Melbourne Cup in 1861 and again in 1862. The family estate and its racing connections give the village a place in a story known well beyond the Shoalhaven.

Terara today

Historic cottages and village homes sit within a landscape that also includes larger rural holdings and riverfront properties. It is a place where two nearby addresses can offer very different land sizes, improvements and lifestyles.

What the property figures actually show

Pricefinder reports two recorded house sales in the latest rolling 12-month period and a median of $1,570,000. Its reported median change is +22.4%, calculated from just four sales across two rolling periods. With such a small sample, that percentage should not be interpreted as proof that comparable Terara homes have risen by 22.4%.

The six supplied advertised listings range from a village home on approximately 1,031 square metres to substantial acreage and riverfront properties, including asking prices around $2.45 million, $2.55 million, $2.58 million and $4 million. One listing did not display a price in the supplied snapshot.

Some recent lower-priced transactions displayed by property portals are dwellings within a residential park. They are not directly comparable to freehold village homes or acreage, and should not be mixed into an interpretation of detached-house values.

Daniel's take

What stands out to me is how much variety exists within a small market. Terara's history and rural character are part of its appeal, but the value of an individual property still comes down to its specific land, improvements, location and buyer demand.

When only two house sales underpin a median, I would rather explain the comparable properties than quote a growth percentage without context. That is the point of Around the Suburbs: to look at the place, not just the number.
