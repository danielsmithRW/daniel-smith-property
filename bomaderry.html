AROUND THE SUBURBS • BOMADERRY

More Than Nowra’s
Northern Neighbour.

Bomaderry is often grouped together with Nowra, but anyone who lives there will tell you it has an identity of its own.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

Bomaderry is often grouped together with Nowra, but anyone who lives there will tell you it has an identity of its own.

It’s a suburb built around connection with the railway, long-standing local industry, schools, sporting clubs and established neighbourhoods where people have often lived for generations.

Connection and convenience

Part of Bomaderry’s appeal is its convenience. You have the railway station, local shopping, schools, sporting facilities and straightforward access to Nowra, Berry and the Princes Highway, without living directly in the centre of town.

The housing is just as varied. Bomaderry offers everything from older cottages and established family homes to villas, units and newer residential pockets. That variety attracts first-home buyers, families, investors, downsizers and people relocating to the area, but it also means one suburb-wide median never tells the complete story.

12 MONTHS TO AUGUST 2026$799,500

Median house sale price across 131 sales. Three-bedroom median: $720,000. Four-bedroom median: $848,750.

That difference is important. Bedrooms matter, but so do the condition of the home, land size, location, layout and the type of buyer it is likely to attract.

Bomaderry is also continuing to change.

Construction of the new Bomaderry Public Preschool is nearing completion, with expressions of interest open for local families at the time this edition was published.

Near the railway station, 60 new build-to-rent apartments are also progressing, including 12 affordable homes, with the first residents expected in early 2027.

Those projects will bring more housing and services into the suburb while continuing the conversation around infrastructure, traffic and how Bomaderry grows without losing what people already value about it.
