NOWRA REGION PROPERTY UPDATE • SEPTEMBER 2026

Spring arrived.
So did more choice.

September showed a market with buyers in it — but not buyers rushing at everything. Across the Nowra Region, the better question wasn't simply “are buyers active?” It was “what are they choosing to act on?”

THE MONTH IN ONE LINE

Buyers were present — but sellers still had to earn their attention.

THE BIG PICTURE

What September looked like.

September marked the beginning of spring, traditionally a period when more property comes to market and buyers have more options to compare. Publicly reported sales across the core Nowra Region showed activity across a broad range of price points — from units and conventional residential homes through to larger lifestyle properties.

Rather than manufacture a “monthly median” from an incomplete public sample, I think the more useful approach is to look at verified local results alongside what buyers were actually doing through open homes and negotiations.

What does that actually tell us?

Across 128 open homes, our team met 151 buyers — an average of 1.18 buyers per open. At the same time, 13 properties moved into conditional status and seven exchanged during the reporting period.

To me, that's an important distinction. Lower inspection numbers don't necessarily mean there aren't buyers in the market. Properties were still transacting, but buyers had choice and were taking the time to compare what was available.

Buyers were present — but sellers still had to earn their attention.

On the rental side, 24 leases were signed during September, showing continued movement across the local rental market as well.

WHAT I'M SEEING ON THE GROUND

Activity and urgency aren't the same thing.

There are buyers in the market. They're attending open homes, making enquiries and watching new properties as they come online.

But they're also comparing.

When a property is positioned correctly and buyers can see the value, we're seeing engagement. When there's a gap between where buyers see value and where a property is positioned, that hesitation becomes much more noticeable.

The first few weeks of a campaign tell us an enormous amount. Online engagement, enquiries, inspections and feedback collectively start painting a picture of where the market sees a property.

The market will usually tell us something — our job is to listen to it.

FOR PROPERTY OWNERS

Look at the pattern, not one sale.

If you're thinking about selling — whether that's next month or next year — don't judge your property's likely result from one nearby transaction. Look at what is competing with you, how long comparable homes are taking to sell, whether asking prices are being adjusted and, importantly, what buyers are actually responding to.

Sometimes the best preparation for a future sale starts months before the signboard goes up.

FOR BUYERS

Use the data. Don't be ruled by it.

Buyers have more information available than ever: previous sales, estimated values, comparable properties and market history. That's useful, but no two homes are identical.

Condition, location within a suburb, land, renovations, layout and competition from other buyers can all affect where a property ultimately sells.

Statistics tell us what happened.

What I find equally valuable is what I'm hearing from buyers at open homes, the conversations I'm having with local property owners, where enquiry is coming from and where properties are gaining traction — or aren't.

That's what I want these monthly updates to be about. Not another generic real estate newsletter, but a genuine look at what's happening across our local market from someone working in it every day.
