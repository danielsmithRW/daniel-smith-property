WEST NOWRA • 21 OCTOBER 2026

Established living
with growth on the horizon.

A smaller established suburb close to Nowra, with university education, natural surrounds and significant residential growth planned around Mundamia.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

Located just west of the Nowra CBD, West Nowra offers established residential living, convenient access to town and close proximity to bushland and recreation. With relatively few properties changing hands each year, it can sometimes fly under the radar compared with larger surrounding suburbs.

But with new housing and infrastructure investment planned around West Nowra and Mundamia, there is much more happening here than first meets the eye.

WEST NOWRA MARKET SNAPSHOT$795,000

Median house price, with 19 house sales in the past 12 months and a median 30 days on market. Median house rent: $550 per week. Indicative rental yield: 4.1%.

A small market where the individual property matters

With a relatively small number of properties selling each year, suburb-wide statistics should be treated as a general guide rather than an indication of what every property may be worth.

Street, land size, presentation, renovations, accommodation and other improvements can make a considerable difference to how buyers respond to an individual West Nowra property.

Living in West Nowra

West Nowra remains only a short drive from the shopping, employment, medical and professional services available throughout Nowra.

The suburb also sits close to natural and recreational areas including Flatrock Dam, Triplarina Nature Reserve and surrounding bushland and walking areas, providing an interesting balance between access to town and the natural environment along Nowra’s western edge.

Education close to home

An important feature of the West Nowra and Mundamia area is the University of Wollongong’s Shoalhaven Campus on George Evans Road.

The campus provides local access to university education and includes teaching spaces, clinical learning facilities, a library, computer labs and dedicated study areas. Its presence adds another dimension to the area, particularly as residential development continues around Mundamia.

What’s ahead for West Nowra?

West Nowra is positioned for significant residential growth over the coming years.

The Mundamia and Cabbage Tree Lane Urban Release Areas have been identified for future residential development, with infrastructure investment intended to help unlock approximately 2,000 new homes across the West Nowra area.

Supporting infrastructure is already progressing. Major wastewater works are being delivered through the West Nowra Urban Release Area, including new sewer infrastructure and a new pumping station at Mundamia. Road infrastructure along the Yalwal Road corridor is also being upgraded to support future development and increased traffic associated with growth in the area.

Individual residential developments are progressing as well, including approved subdivision and multi-dwelling development around the Depot Road and Rainford Road area.

Development will occur progressively, but over the longer term these projects have the potential to significantly change West Nowra and Mundamia — increasing housing supply, expanding the residential community and bringing additional infrastructure investment into the area.

A suburb worth watching

West Nowra is an interesting example of an established suburb sitting alongside one of the broader Nowra area’s future growth corridors.

Today it remains a relatively small residential market. Over the coming years, new housing, infrastructure investment and continued development around Mundamia could see the surrounding area look considerably different.

Daniel’s take

For West Nowra homeowners, the suburb median is useful context, but with only a limited number of annual sales it tells only part of the story. The more useful question is where your particular home sits against the properties buyers are currently comparing it with.

Condition, improvements, land, position and buyer appeal will always provide more context than a suburb-wide figure alone.
