BUYERS • DANIEL'S TAKE

What are buyers actually looking at when they make an offer?

Buyers rarely look at the asking price in isolation. They are working out what the property will cost them after they own it.

They are doing the maths beyond the purchase price.

A buyer can love a property and still reduce what they are prepared to offer because they see flooring to replace, paint to do, an older kitchen, landscaping, maintenance or larger future expenses. Those numbers become part of their decision.

Presentation changes the first calculation.

This is why two similar homes can get very different reactions. Layout, natural light, condition, land, storage, presentation and how easily buyers can imagine living there all influence the conversation. Bedrooms and square metres matter, but they are not the entire property.

Buyers don't just ask, “What is it worth?” They ask, “What will I need to spend after I buy it?”

Feedback matters during a campaign.

One buyer's opinion is just one opinion. When the same concern appears repeatedly, it becomes useful market information. A good campaign should keep listening, identify patterns and respond rather than simply waiting for another open home.
