NORTH NOWRA • 8 OCTOBER 2026

Why established
still matters.

North Nowra’s appeal isn’t built around being the newest suburb. In many ways, its strength is that it’s already established.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

For many people, North Nowra offers a practical balance: established neighbourhoods, schools, local shopping and sporting facilities, while still being close to Nowra’s CBD, the Shoalhaven River and surrounding bushland.

It’s a suburb people often move into for a particular stage of life, then find reasons to stay.

An established suburb with different pockets

North Nowra isn’t one uniform property market. The suburb includes original homes, renovated family properties, villas, units and larger homes in elevated or quieter residential pockets.

That gives buyers a range of options, but it also means two properties with the same number of bedrooms can achieve very different results. Presentation, renovation, land, layout, outlook and the exact location within the suburb all influence how buyers respond.

The numbers behind the headline

North Nowra’s median house price increased by 9.3% over the 12-month period. That’s a healthy headline result, but it doesn’t mean every property automatically increased by the same amount. The types of homes sold during a particular period can shift the suburb median in either direction.

Three-bedroom houses recorded a median sale price of $760,000, based on 48 sales, and took a median of 30 days to sell. Four-bedroom houses recorded a median of $850,533 across 45 sales, with a median of 34 days on market.

That difference isn’t simply the value of an additional bedroom. Renovation, usable land, garaging, living space, presentation and the buyer group a home appeals to can all have a considerable effect on the result.

The median advertised house rent was $630 per week, based on 81 leases during the same period. Rental houses took a median of 22 days to lease.

What is changing locally?

North Nowra is established, but that doesn’t mean it’s standing still.

Almost $1 million in NSW Government funding has been secured for sporting infrastructure across North Nowra and Bomaderry. The Bernie Regan Sporting Complex is among the facilities identified to receive future upgrades to tennis, hockey and sporting infrastructure.

Council has also endorsed a draft plan that could allow the former North Nowra landfill site to become a community renewable-energy hub incorporating solar generation and battery storage.

The renewable-energy hub remains an early-stage proposal. Further consultation and statutory processes are required before the site could be leased to an energy provider. It is still an interesting example of how land with limited alternative uses could potentially become a productive community asset.

Daniel’s take

North Nowra continues to appeal because much of what buyers look for is already there. The suburb is established, connected and familiar—but the individual properties within it can differ considerably.

For homeowners, the suburb median is useful context. The more important question is where your particular home sits against the properties buyers are currently comparing it with.

Condition, improvements, land, position and buyer appeal will always tell you more than a suburb-wide percentage alone.
