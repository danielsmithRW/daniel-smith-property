NOWRA • 14 OCTOBER 2026

More than just
the centre of town.

Nowra is the heart of the Shoalhaven, but from a property perspective it is far more varied than a single suburb-wide number suggests.

By Daniel SmithRay White Shoalhaven Central Group

When most people think of Nowra, they think of the CBD, Stockland, the highway and probably traffic. But the suburb itself is a mix of established residential streets, older homes on generous blocks, renovated properties, units, investment stock and sites where zoning or development potential can completely change the way a property is viewed.

It is one of those markets where the street, the block and the property itself matter enormously.

A centre that is still changing

Nowra may be established, but there is still plenty happening around it.

One of the biggest long-term opportunities is the Nowra Riverfront Precinct. Planning and design work is progressing on the area along the Shoalhaven River, with the riverfront identified as an opportunity for a stronger civic, community, tourism and recreational role.

The proposed East Nowra Sub-Arterial Road is also being designed to create an alternative connection between Greenwell Point Road and the Princes Highway at North Street, intended to improve connections between the CBD, Worrigee and the coastal villages while taking pressure off the existing Kalandar Street route.

Better walking and cycling connections are part of the picture too, including the shared-user path along Old Southern Road between Greenwell Point Road and Peppermint Drive.

Within the CBD, funding has also been directed toward improvements around the Stewart Place Bus Transit Precinct and surrounding laneways through the NSW Government’s Safer Cities: Her Way program.

12 MONTHS TO SEPTEMBER 2026 $715,000

Median house sale price based on 183 sales. Houses took a median of 40 days to sell.

The numbers behind the headline

Nowra’s median house price increased by 5.5% over the 12-month period. As always, that is useful for understanding the direction of the wider market, but it does not mean every Nowra property increased by the same amount.

Three-bedroom houses recorded a median of $670,000, while four-bedroom homes reached $782,500.

That is already more than a $110,000 difference before considering renovation, presentation, block size, location or zoning.

The median advertised house rent was $560 per week over the same period.

Why Nowra needs context

A neat three-bedroom home bought by a first-home buyer is a very different proposition to an older property sitting on land with development potential. Likewise, a renovated home in a tightly held street may attract a completely different level of competition to another property only a few blocks away.

Nowra includes buyers looking for convenience, investors, first-home buyers, downsizers, families and people whose purchasing decision may be influenced as much by the land and zoning as the house itself.

That variety is exactly why a suburb median can only take you so far.

Daniel’s take

The $715,000 median tells us what the broader Nowra market is doing. It does not automatically tell a homeowner what their property is worth.

Street matters. Land size matters. Condition matters. Zoning can matter enormously. And most importantly, what buyers are actually comparing and competing for at the time matters.

For a Nowra homeowner, the useful question is not simply “What is the suburb median?” It is “Where does my property sit within this market?”
