There's something about Greenwell Point that makes it feel a little different from the rest of the Shoalhaven. Perhaps it's the fishing boats along the Crookhaven River, the fresh oysters or the slower pace of life that comes with living in a coastal village.

A village shaped by the water

For locals and visitors alike, the water is part of everyday life. Fishing, boating, the foreshore and time spent outdoors help give Greenwell Point its character. The waterfront playground and picnic spots offer families a place to enjoy that setting, too.

Food is part of the local story. Pelican Rocks Café is known for its award-winning fish and chips, while family-owned Jim Wild's Oysters brings the area's oyster-farming tradition directly to the table.

A surprisingly varied property market

It can be tempting to think of Greenwell Point as one coastal housing market. The current listings show why that would be too simple. The 21 properties advertised for sale in the local check range from a $350,000 permanent caravan park site to waterfront land asking $2.5 million.

For an established residential comparison, a waterfront reserve property is advertised at $2.25 million to $2.3 million. That is an asking range, not a recorded sale. The difference between a caravan park site, vacant land, an ordinary home and a waterfront reserve home matters when considering prices.

What the sales tell us

There have been 25 reported sales over the past 12 months, ranging from $425,000 for vacant land to $1,825,000 for a residential property with water views but no direct waterfront position.

The approximate median sale price is $850,000, and the reported days on market sit around 73 to 78 days. Those numbers provide a useful starting point, but they do not tell us whether an individual property is correctly priced or why a particular home sold quickly or slowly.

With such a broad mix of property types, the median should not be mistaken for the price of a typical detached house. A site's tenure, the improvements on the land, its outlook and its relationship to the water can all make a substantial difference.

Daniel's take

What stands out to me isn't just the difference between the highest and lowest prices. It's the difference between what those properties actually offer. Someone buying a permanent caravan park site is looking at a different proposition from someone considering a waterfront reserve residence.

Greenwell Point is a reminder that a suburb is more than its median. The lifestyle draws people in, but the land, the property and the comparable sales still need to be considered individually.
