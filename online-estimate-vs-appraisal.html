APPRAISALS • DANIEL'S TAKE

Online estimate vs a real property appraisal.

Online estimates can be a useful starting point when you're curious about your property's value. The important part is understanding what that number is based on, what it may be missing and when a closer look can give you better context.

What is an online property estimate actually doing?

Most automated estimates use available property records, previous sales and nearby sales data to calculate an estimated value. That can be useful when you want a quick indication without speaking to anyone. But it is still a data-based estimate. The system is working with the information it has available, and that information may not tell the full story of your home today.

An algorithm has not walked through your front door.

Two homes can look very similar in property data and still attract very different buyer responses. An automated estimate may not fully account for changes or characteristics such as:

  • a renovated kitchen or bathroom
  • extensions, additional living areas or a granny flat
  • large sheds, workshops or improved outdoor spaces
  • the condition and presentation of the home
  • views, outlook, privacy or position within the street
  • floorplan, natural light and how the home actually feels to walk through
  • improvements that have occurred since the available property data was last updated

Those details do not automatically add a set dollar amount. What matters is how they compare with other properties available to buyers and whether buyers are willing to pay more for them.

Why can different online estimates give you different numbers?

Different platforms can use different data sources, models and assumptions. One may place more weight on recent nearby sales, while another may rely more heavily on historical property information or broader suburb trends. If the underlying information is incomplete or the property is unusual for the area, the range between estimates can become even wider.

That does not necessarily mean one estimate is right and another is wrong. It is a reminder that the number is a modelled estimate, not an inspection of the property.

When is an online estimate useful?

There is nothing wrong with using one. They can be helpful when you're at the very beginning of thinking about your property and simply want a rough reference point. They can also give you a quick sense of how values in your area may have moved since you purchased.

The mistake is treating the estimate as a valuation of the home itself rather than a starting point for further research.

Comparable sales still need context.

A nearby sale is not automatically a good comparable. The most useful sales are the ones that help explain what buyers have recently paid for properties that genuinely compete with yours.

That means looking beyond the final sale price. Land size, condition, bedrooms, renovations, presentation, street position, buyer competition, the original asking price and how the campaign performed can all help explain why one property sold for more or less than another.

Data gives us a starting point. The property itself gives the number context.

The most recent sale isn't always the best comparison.

A house that sold around the corner last week may seem like the obvious benchmark, but another property sold two months ago could actually be more relevant if its land, accommodation, condition and buyer appeal are closer to yours. Good comparable sales are about similarity and buyer behaviour, not simply distance or date.

So what does a property appraisal add?

An appraisal brings the data and the actual property together. It allows the home to be considered in its current condition, including improvements, presentation and features that may not appear in an automated model. It also allows recent comparable sales to be considered alongside current competition and what buyers are responding to in the market now.

The result should not just be a number. You should understand the reasoning behind the suggested range, which properties are being used as comparisons and what may influence where your property sits within that range.

You don't need to be selling to ask.

Knowing where your property sits can be useful long before you decide to sell. You might be considering renovations, planning an upgrade, reviewing an investment, thinking about refinancing or simply wondering whether the improvements you've made have changed the picture.

Sometimes the useful outcome is not a decision to sell. It is simply having better information for whatever you decide to do next.

THE SIMPLE VERSION

Every property is different.

An online estimate is a useful place to start. A closer appraisal becomes valuable when you want to understand the property itself, the sales buyers are genuinely comparing it with and what is happening in the local market right now.
